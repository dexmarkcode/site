# Glossary

Age uke
:   Rising block. At close range, often a cover entry, elbow lift or forearm strike.

Bunkai
:   Analysis of kata; the study of what the movements are for.

Chi'ishi
:   A traditional conditioning tool: a weight on the end of a wooden handle, used to strengthen the wrists, forearms and grip.

Dachi
:   Stance, as in *sanchin dachi* or *shiko dachi*.

Fumikomi
:   Stamping kick, driving the heel or sole down into the foot, shin or knee.

Gedan barai
:   Downward sweeping block. At close range, an arm pass, hammer fist or low sweep.

Go
:   Hard. The structural, connected, forceful side of the art.

Go no sen
:   Late initiative: responding after the attack has been launched.

Goju-Ryu
:   The "hard-soft school" of karate, named by Chojun Miyagi around 1930.

Hiji-ate
:   Elbow strike. Also *empi*.

Hikite
:   The pulling hand that returns to the hip. In application, a hand that has caught something and is pulling it.

Hiza-geri
:   Knee strike.

Hojo undo
:   Supplementary training with traditional tools for strength and conditioning.

Ibuki
:   Forceful, audible breathing, as used in Sanchin.

Ju
:   Soft. The sensitive, yielding, adaptable side of the art.

Kaishu kata
:   The "open hand" kata of Goju-Ryu, as distinct from the training kata Sanchin and Tensho.

Kake
:   In throwing, the execution: the throwing action itself.

Kake uke
:   Hooking block. At close range, a hook over an arm that pulls it into control.

Kakie
:   The Goju-Ryu "sticky hands" partner exercise for developing sensitivity, pushing and yielding with the forearms in contact.

Kansetsu-waza
:   Joint techniques: locks and controls applied to the joints.

Kata
:   A prescribed sequence of movements that records and transmits the techniques of a style.

Kote kitae
:   Forearm conditioning by striking forearms together with a partner.

Kumite
:   Sparring.

Kuzushi
:   Balance-breaking: moving someone's centre of gravity towards or beyond the edge of their base.

Ma-ai
:   Distance or interval between two people.

Makiwara
:   A padded striking post used to condition the hands and develop focused power.

Mawashi uke
:   Circular block, also called *tora guchi* ("tiger's mouth"). Contains a pass, a pin and a double push or strike.

Muchimi
:   An Okinawan term for a heavy, sticky, rubbery quality in the arms: soft on the surface and strong beneath.

Nage-waza
:   Throwing techniques.

Nigiri game
:   Gripping jars: weighted pots held by the rim to build grip and posture.

Oyo
:   Practical application of a technique.

Rei
:   Bow; also courtesy and respect.

Sanchin
:   The fundamental Goju-Ryu training kata for structure, breathing and tension; also the stance used in it.

Sen no sen
:   Simultaneous initiative: countering as the attack develops.

Sen sen no sen
:   Pre-emptive initiative: acting as the intention to attack forms.

Shiko dachi
:   Low straddle stance, prominent in Seiyunchin.

Shime
:   Testing of structure by pushing, pressing and striking a person performing Sanchin.

Shotei
:   Palm-heel strike.

Suri ashi
:   Sliding footwork in which the feet never cross.

Tai sabaki
:   Body movement: turning and shifting the body off the line.

Tanden
:   The lower abdomen, a little below the navel; the centre of breathing and balance.

Tenshin
:   Pivoting movement that turns the body off the line without opening the distance.

Tensho
:   The soft counterpart to Sanchin, with flowing, circling hand movements.

Tettsui
:   Hammer-fist strike.

Tora guchi
:   "Tiger's mouth"; see *mawashi uke*.

Tsukuri
:   In throwing, the entry: fitting your body into position.

Tuidi (tuite)
:   Okinawan methods of seizing and manipulating the joints.

Uchikomi
:   Repetitive entry practice for throws, without completing the throw.

Ukemi
:   Breakfalling.

Uraken
:   Back-fist strike.
