# How to Use This Book

## Who this book is for

This book is written for three kinds of reader.

*The Goju-Ryu practitioner* who knows the kata and wants to understand what they are teaching at close range. You will find the kata linked to practical applications throughout, and a map of those links at the back.

*The karateka of any style* who keeps being smothered when sparring closes up, or who wants to add close-range skills to a long-range game. The principles of structure, sensitivity, trapping and short striking apply to every style.

*The reader interested in self-protection* who wants a sensible, honest guide to the distance at which most real violence happens. You do not need to have trained in Goju-Ryu to benefit, although you will need a training partner and, ideally, a teacher.

## How the book is organised

The book has six parts, each with three chapters, and they are meant to be read in order. Each part builds on the ones before it.

- **Part One: The Close-Range Foundations** covers the context of the art, Sanchin structure, and the geometry of distance and angles.
- **Part Two: Sensitivity and Trapping** covers kakie, the traps that grow out of it, and the combinations that turn traps into finished exchanges.
- **Part Three: The Short Weapons** covers elbows, knees and the clinch.
- **Part Four: Throws, Takedowns and the Ground** covers balance-breaking, the throws in the kata, and breakfalling, ground survival and getting up.
- **Part Five: Joint Controls and Restraint** covers the principles of joint control, specific controls, and escapes from grabs and holds.
- **Part Six: Counter-Attack, Sparring and the Real World** covers timing, progressive sparring, and real-world awareness, law and aftermath.

At the back you will find a twelve-week training programme, a map of kata applications, frequently asked questions and a glossary.

## How each chapter works

Every chapter follows the same problem-solving pattern:

1. **The problem**: what is happening and why it is dangerous.
2. **The principle**: the general idea that solves it.
3. **The technique**: the specific movements, with diagrams.
4. **The drill**: how to practise, from cooperative to resisting.
5. **Pressure**: how to test it safely.

You will also find several kinds of boxed material.

::: drill
**Drill boxes** give partner and solo exercises, with clear progressions.
:::

::: kata
**Kata links** connect the technique to movements in the Goju-Ryu kata.
:::

::: fault
**Common faults** describe the errors I see most often and how to correct them.
:::

::: street
**Street notes** highlight the differences between training and real-world encounters, including legal points.
:::

Each chapter ends with a **Problem-Solving Clinic**, which answers the difficulties students most often run into, a summary of **Key Points**, and a short set of **Training Questions** to take into the dojo.

## Reading the diagrams

The diagrams follow simple conventions, explained in detail in Chapter 1.

- **You** are drawn in **solid black**.
- **The opponent or partner** is drawn in **white with a black outline**.
- **Far-side limbs** are drawn in **grey**, so you can tell the near arm or leg from the far one.
- **Solid arrows** show the main movement; **dashed arrows** show a preparation or secondary movement.
- A **starburst** marks a point of impact.
- **Numbered circles** show the order of events within one picture; **numbered panels** show a sequence.
- **Footprint and overhead diagrams** show footwork and positions from above, with your feet or body in black and the other person's in white.

The figures are deliberately simple. They show where the body goes and which way the force travels. Read the text alongside them, then try the movement slowly with a partner.

## How to train with this book

*Go slowly.* Every technique should first be practised at walking pace with a cooperative partner. Speed and resistance come later.

*Follow the progressions.* The drills are arranged from cooperative to resisting for a reason. Skipping stages is the fastest route to injury and frustration.

*Use the twelve-week programme.* If you are not sure where to begin, the programme at the back of the book takes you through the material in a sensible order, with suggested sessions for each week.

*Train with a teacher.* A book cannot watch you and correct your mistakes. A good teacher can. Use this book alongside regular instruction, not instead of it.

*Keep a diary.* Note what you practised, what worked and what did not. Over months, the diary becomes a record of your progress and a guide to what to work on next.

## Safety

Please read the safety and legal notice at the front of the book before you begin. In particular:

- never practise throws or falls without proper mats and competent breakfalls;
- never apply joint controls quickly, and always release instantly on the tap;
- never apply pressure to a partner's neck;
- never strike the head, throat, groin, knees or spine with force in training;
- wear appropriate protective equipment for any contact work;
- stop if you feel pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness.
