# Introduction: The Distance Where Fights Are Decided

Close your eyes for a moment and picture a fight. Not a film fight, not a competition, but the kind of thing that happens outside a takeaway at midnight, in a car park after a football match, or in a hallway when an argument gets out of hand.

What do you see? Almost certainly not two people bouncing on their toes at kicking distance, trading neat techniques. You see two people very close together. Someone is shouting into someone else's face. There is a push, a grab at a jacket, a wild swing, then both of them are wrapped up in each other, pulling, punching at short range, stumbling into walls, perhaps falling to the ground.

That is the close range. It is where most real violence happens, and it is where a surprising amount of sport fighting is decided too. It is also, for a great many trained martial artists, the place where their training stops working.

I have met black belts with beautiful kata and fast, accurate sparring who froze completely when someone grabbed their collar. I have met competition champions who were bundled around the mat by untrained but aggressive partners the moment the distance closed. I have met kind, capable people who had trained for years and still had no real idea what to do if someone put their hands on them. None of this was their fault. They had simply never been taught the close range in a way that made sense.

## Why Goju-Ryu?

Goju-Ryu is, in my view, one of the best-equipped traditional arts for close combat. Its origins in southern Chinese boxing, its emphasis on structure through Sanchin and sensitivity through kakie, its short and powerful strikes, and the throws, joint controls and grappling movements hidden in its kata all point to the close range as its natural home.

But knowing that the material is there is not the same as being able to use it. Many Goju-Ryu practitioners perform kata full of close-range applications without ever being shown what those applications are, or being given a way to train them against a resisting partner. The kata remain beautiful shapes rather than practical skills.

This book is an attempt to close that gap.

## What This Book Will Give You

By the end of this book, if you train the material properly, you should be able to:

- stand and move with structure that holds when someone pushes, pulls or leans on you;
- manage distance and angles so that you arrive at close range in a safe position, off the other person's line;
- feel what another person is doing through contact, and respond before you see it;
- trap and control their arms to open lines for your own strikes;
- use elbows and knees with power and precision, and string them into combinations;
- hold your own in the clinch, control the head and posture, and get off a wall;
- break balance and throw, using movements from the kata, and fall safely yourself;
- control, steer and restrain with joint controls when a strike would be wrong;
- escape from grabs, holds and neck attacks;
- time your counter-attacks to the other person's actions;
- spar at close range safely and productively; and
- understand awareness, de-escalation, the law and the aftermath of real encounters.

That is a great deal, and it will not happen quickly. Close-range skill is built slowly, over months and years. But each chapter gives you something you can use immediately, and the skills accumulate.

## A Problem-Solving Book

You will notice that this book is organised around problems. That is deliberate. Techniques on their own are answers without questions. If you know thirty elbow strikes but not which situation each one solves, you will not be able to find the right one when you need it.

So every chapter starts with a problem that people really face: being pushed backwards, being grabbed, being smothered, being held from behind. It then gives you the principle that solves that kind of problem, the techniques that apply it, the drills that build it and the pressure tests that prove it. And it ends with a clinic of the difficulties students most often encounter when they train it, with the solutions that have worked for my students.

If you are stuck, look at the clinic first. Someone has almost certainly been stuck in the same place before you.

## A Word About Honesty

Martial arts books have a long tradition of exaggeration. Techniques are described as unstoppable, masters as invincible, secrets as ancient and infallible. I have tried hard to avoid all of that.

Nothing in this book is unstoppable. Every technique can fail, and many will fail the first hundred times you try them against a resisting partner. Some of the kata interpretations I offer may not be what the creators of the kata intended; they are the interpretations that have proved useful under pressure. And none of this will make you invincible. Real violence is chaotic and dangerous, and even the most skilled people get hurt.

What I can promise is that the methods in this book work well enough, often enough, to make a real difference: to your karate, to your sparring and, if it ever comes to it, to your safety. They have done so for me and for many of my students. With patient training, they can do so for you.

Let us begin where the art itself begins: with how Goju-Ryu came to live at the close range.
