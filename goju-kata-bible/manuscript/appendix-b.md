# Appendix B: A Twelve-Week Kata Programme and Training Log

This appendix gives you a structured twelve-week programme that can be applied to any kata in the book, together with a log to record your practice. It is designed to sit alongside your normal dojo classes, not to replace them. Most people will be able to complete each session in thirty to forty minutes.

## How the programme works

The twelve weeks are divided into four blocks of three weeks each. Each block has a different emphasis, following the stages of learning set out in Chapter 3.

- **Weeks 1 to 3: shape and floor.** Learn or revise the sequence and the embusen until they are automatic.
- **Weeks 4 to 6: structure.** Concentrate on stances, hand positions and the line of the body in every count.
- **Weeks 7 to 9: transitions, rhythm and breath.** Work on how you move between positions, and on the tempo and breathing pattern of the kata.
- **Weeks 10 to 12: meaning and performance.** Study the bunkai with a partner and perform the kata under observation.

Each week contains four sessions. If you can only manage three, drop the fourth session of each week rather than shortening the others.

## The standard session

Every session follows the same pattern.

1. **Warm-up (five minutes).** Joint mobility from the neck to the ankles, then light movement to raise the heart rate.
2. **Foundation (five to ten minutes).** The stance routine from Chapter 2, or Sanchin three times.
3. **Main work (fifteen to twenty minutes).** The focus for the week, as set out below.
4. **Whole kata (five minutes).** Perform the complete kata two or three times, whatever the focus of the week.
5. **Cool-down and log (five minutes).** Gentle stretching, then fill in the training log.

## Week-by-week focus

Table: **Twelve-week programme.** Adapt the detail to the kata you are working on.

| Week | Main focus | Specific tasks |
|:--|:------|:------------|
| 1 | Embusen | Walk the floor pattern ten times without techniques, calling directions aloud |
| 2 | Sequence | Add stances, then hands, section by section, checking against the diagrams |
| 3 | Memory | Count-sheet test; reverse practice; mirror practice of section one |
| 4 | Stances | Hold each stance of the kata for thirty seconds before moving on |
| 5 | Hand positions | Stop on each count and check height, angle and hikite in a mirror |
| 6 | Body line | Video from the side; check spine, hips and head on every count |
| 7 | Transitions | Isolate the three hardest transitions; ten repetitions each |
| 8 | Rhythm | Mark slow, fast and pause points on the count sheet; perform to them |
| 9 | Breath | Breath-only practice; then full kata with exaggerated breathing |
| 10 | Bunkai | Two sessions with a partner on two sections of the kata |
| 11 | Performance | Perform for an audience each session; use the Appendix A checklist |
| 12 | Polish and taper | Reduce volume; one mock grading; rest the day before any grading |

## Notes on each block

### Weeks 1 to 3

Most early mistakes in a kata are floor-pattern mistakes. Do not be impatient to add techniques. A student who can walk the embusen perfectly, calling out the direction of each count, has done half the work of learning the kata. By the end of week three you should be able to pass the count-sheet test, covering the right-hand columns of the table in the kata's chapter and naming every count correctly.

### Weeks 4 to 6

This block is slow and can feel tedious. It is also where the greatest improvements are made. Use a mirror if you have one, and video if you can. Compare each count with the diagram panel. Pay particular attention to the faults listed in the kata's problems and solutions section, because they are the faults you are most likely to have.

### Weeks 7 to 9

Now the kata begins to come alive. The transitions are where power is generated and lost; the rhythm and breath give the kata its character. Read the breathing, rhythm and timing section of the kata's chapter again before week eight. If you have a training partner, ask them to time your performance and compare it with the typical durations given in the chapter.

### Weeks 10 to 12

Bunkai should be practised only with a willing partner, under supervision, slowly and with control. Its purpose here is not to make you a fighter but to make your kata precise: once you know what a movement is for, you will perform it with intent. Performance practice in front of others is the single best preparation for a grading. Use the Appendix A checklist and take the observer's comments seriously.

## Reflection after twelve weeks

When the twelve weeks are over, take half an hour to read your log from beginning to end and answer these questions in writing.

1. Which three faults in this kata have I corrected?
2. Which fault is still there?
3. Which section of the kata do I now perform best, and which worst?
4. What do I understand about the meaning of this kata that I did not understand twelve weeks ago?
5. What will I work on in the next twelve weeks?

Keep your answers with your log. When you return to this kata in a year's time, you will be glad you did.
