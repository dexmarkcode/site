# Glossary

Age empi / age hiji
:   Rising elbow strike, driving upward under the chin or into the head.

Age uke (jōdan age uke)
:   Rising block that protects the head, the forearm finishing above and in front of the forehead.

Bunkai
:   Analysis of a kata; the study of the applications of its movements.

Chakugan
:   Placing the eyes; the principle that the eyes lead every turn and movement.

Chūdan
:   Middle level, from the collarbones to the belt.

Chūdan uke
:   Middle-level block; in Gōjū-ryū the characteristic block of Sanchin, with the forearm vertical.

Dan
:   Black-belt grade, counted upwards from shodan (first dan).

Dōjō kun
:   The precepts of the dojo, often recited at the end of class.

Embusen
:   The line of performance; the floor pattern of a kata.

Empi uchi
:   Elbow strike. Mae empi is forward, yoko empi is to the side.

Fukyūgata
:   Promotional kata; in Gōjū-ryū, the two Gekisai kata.

Fumikomi
:   Stamping kick, driving down with the heel or the edge of the foot.

Gedan
:   Lower level, below the belt.

Gedan barai
:   Downward sweeping block.

Gō
:   Hard; one half of the name Gōjū-ryū.

Gyaku tsuki
:   Reverse punch, thrown with the hand opposite to the forward leg.

Hachiji dachi
:   Natural stance with the feet shoulder width apart and the toes slightly turned out.

Heiko dachi
:   Parallel stance, feet shoulder width apart and toes forward.

Heishugata
:   Closed-hand kata: Sanchin and Tenshō.

Hiki uke
:   Pulling block, finishing with the hand drawing an arm towards you.

Hikite
:   The pulling hand, drawn back to the hip as the other hand strikes or blocks.

Hiza geri
:   Knee strike.

Hojo undō
:   Supplementary conditioning exercises, often with traditional equipment.

Ibuki
:   Power breathing, deep and audible, used in Sanchin and slow sections of kata.

Jōdan
:   Upper level, the head and neck.

Jū
:   Soft; one half of the name Gōjū-ryū.

Kaishu
:   Open hand. Kaishugata are the open-hand kata; kaishu yōi is the open-hand ready position.

Kakie
:   Two-person sticking and pushing-hands exercise.

Kake uke
:   Hooking block with the open hand.

Kakiwake
:   Wedge block, in which the arms part to break a grip or a double attack.

Kansetsu geri
:   Stamping kick to the knee joint.

Kata
:   A prearranged sequence of movements; a form.

Kiai
:   A shout from the abdomen at a moment of maximum focus.

Kihon
:   Basics; fundamental techniques practised alone.

Kime
:   Focus; the instant of total commitment at the end of a technique.

Kōsa dachi
:   Crossed-leg stance.

Kumite
:   Sparring or partner practice.

Kyū
:   Coloured-belt grade, counted downwards towards first kyū.

Mae geri
:   Front kick.

Mawashi uke
:   Circular two-handed block, finishing in tora guchi.

Morote
:   Both hands, as in morote tsuki (double punch) or morote shōtei (double palm strike).

Musubi dachi
:   Formal stance, heels together, toes open.

Naore
:   Return to the starting position.

Neko ashi dachi
:   Cat stance, weight on the back leg and the front heel raised.

Nogare
:   Escape breathing; quieter breathing used in fast movement.

Nukite
:   Spear hand. Yonhon nukite uses four fingers, nihon nukite two.

Osae
:   Pressing down with the palm.

Rei
:   Bow.

Renzuki
:   Continuous punching.

Sagi ashi dachi
:   Heron stance, on one leg.

Sanchin dachi
:   The rooted hourglass stance of Sanchin, the foundation of Gōjū-ryū.

Shiko dachi
:   Wide, low stance with the knees out over the toes.

Shōtei
:   Palm-heel strike.

Sukui uke
:   Scooping block, catching and lifting a kicking leg.

Tanden
:   The centre of the body, below the navel.

Tettsui
:   Hammer fist, striking with the little-finger edge of the fist.

Tora guchi
:   Tiger's mouth; the finishing position of mawashi uke.

Tsuki
:   Thrusting strike; punch.

Uchi
:   Striking technique delivered in an arc.

Uke
:   Receiving technique, usually translated as block. Also the partner who attacks in a drill.

Uraken
:   Back-fist strike.

Yame
:   Stop.

Yōi
:   Ready; the ready position.

Zanshin
:   Remaining mind; continued awareness after a technique or kata ends.

Zenkutsu dachi
:   Forward stance.
