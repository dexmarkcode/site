# Appendix A: Grading Checklists

These checklists are designed to be photocopied (the owner of this book has permission to copy them for personal and dojo use). Give one to a training partner and ask them to mark each point as you perform. Use the three columns for three different practice sessions, so that you can see your progress. A tick means the point was clearly met; a cross means it was not; a question mark means the observer was unsure.

Before each checklist, the observer should note the date, the venue and how many times the kata had already been performed that session. Fatigue changes everything, and a kata that scores well when fresh may fall apart at the end of a long class. Examiners see you at the end of a long grading.
