# Further Reading

The following books will reward any student who wants to go further. They differ in their versions of the kata and in their historical accounts, and reading several of them side by side is an education in itself.

- **Morio Higaonna**, *Traditional Karate-do: Okinawa Goju Ryu*, volumes 1 to 4. The classic photographic reference for the IOGKF versions of the kata, with valuable material on fundamentals and conditioning.
- **Morio Higaonna**, *The History of Karate: Okinawan Goju-Ryu*. A detailed history of the style from the IOGKF perspective.
- **Patrick McCarthy** (translator), *Bubishi: The Classic Manual of Combat*. An annotated English translation of the text from which the name Gōjū-ryū was taken.
- **Iain Abernethy**, *Bunkai-Jutsu: The Practical Application of Karate Kata*. A thoughtful approach to applied bunkai that is not style-specific.
- **Kris Wilder**, *The Way of Sanchin Kata*. A focused study of Sanchin and its role in the system.
- **Mark Bishop**, *Okinawan Karate: Teachers, Styles and Secret Techniques*. A survey of Okinawan styles and their teachers, useful for placing Gōjū-ryū in context.
- **Shōshin Nagamine**, *The Essence of Okinawan Karate-Do*. Written from a Shōrin-ryū perspective, but full of insight into the Okinawan approach to kata.

Your own association will almost certainly have its own syllabus documents, kata handbooks and video material. These should always take precedence over any published book, including this one, when you prepare for a grading.
