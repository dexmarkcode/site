# The Twelve-Week Sanchin Strength Programme

::: chapter-intro
This chapter brings together the kata practice of Parts Two to Seven and the supplementary training of Chapters 22 and 23 into a single, structured programme. It runs for twelve weeks in three phases, with two kinds of session, clear progressions for every exercise, a mobility routine, planned lighter weeks and reassessment points. It also explains how to adjust the programme if you are short of time, older, recovering from injury or already well conditioned.
:::

Many students who take up supplementary training do it in bursts. They read about the nigiri game, buy some buckets, carry them up and down the garden for a week, then forget about them. A month later they discover planks, and do those for a fortnight. Nothing is wrong with any of these exercises. What is missing is structure: a plan that tells you what to do on which day, how to make it harder over time, when to rest and how to tell whether it is working.

The programme in this chapter provides that structure. It is designed for a student who trains in the dojo once or twice a week and can add four or five short sessions at home. It takes about twelve weeks, and at the end of it most students find that their Sanchin holds its shape far longer, survives testing far better and feels far less effortful.

It is a template, not a prescription. Adjust it to your life, your body and your teacher's advice. But do follow it for the full twelve weeks before deciding whether it works. Strength and endurance take time to build, and the most noticeable changes often come in the second half.

## Overview

The programme has three phases of four weeks each.

**Phase 1, weeks 1 to 4: Foundation.** The emphasis is on learning the exercises, building basic endurance, improving mobility and establishing the habit. Loads are light; Sanchin is practised at Stage 2 breathing and moderate tension.

**Phase 2, weeks 5 to 8: Build.** Loads, hold times and repetitions increase. The hojo circuit becomes more demanding. Sanchin moves to Stage 3 breathing, with one harder session a week.

**Phase 3, weeks 9 to 12: Consolidate.** Strength work becomes more Sanchin-specific, with more loaded stance work and more testing. Sanchin is practised at Stages 3 and 4, with regular partner or solo testing.

The fourth week of each phase is a lighter week, in which volume drops by about a third. This lets the body absorb the work of the previous three weeks. The full ten-part assessment from Chapter 22 is repeated at the end of week 6 and week 12.

## The Weekly Pattern

Each week contains two kinds of home session, plus your normal dojo classes and one full rest day.

**Session A: Strength and Sanchin.** About thirty to forty minutes. Warm-up, the hojo circuit, a strength block, then Sanchin practice. Performed three times a week on non-consecutive days.

**Session B: Skill and mobility.** About twenty minutes. Warm-up, mobility routine, Sanchin drills on a chosen focus, then a calm performance of the kata. Performed two or three times a week, on the days between Session A.

A typical week might look like this:

| Day | Session | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | A | Strength and Sanchin |
| Tuesday | Dojo class or B | Skill and mobility |
| Wednesday | A | Strength and Sanchin |
| Thursday | Dojo class or B | Skill and mobility |
| Friday | A | Strength and Sanchin |
| Saturday | B | Skill and mobility; optional partner testing |
| Sunday | Rest | Walk, stretch gently, no hard training |

: **Table 24.1** A typical training week. Move the days to suit your dojo timetable, but keep at least one day between Session A days.

## Session A in Detail

### 1. Warm-up (5–8 minutes)

The warm-up from Chapter 22.

### 2. Hojo circuit (8–12 minutes)

The ten-minute circuit from Drill 23.1. One round in Phase 1, two rounds in Phases 2 and 3, with a minute's rest between rounds.

### 3. Strength block (12–15 minutes)

Choose one exercise from each of the five groups below, at the level indicated for the current phase. Perform them as a circuit: one set of each in turn, then repeat. Two rounds in Phase 1, three in Phases 2 and 3. Rest about a minute between rounds.

| Group | Level 1 (Phase 1) | Level 2 (Phase 2) | Level 3 (Phase 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legs | Wall sit at Sanchin angle, 30–45 s | Wall sit 60–90 s; or split squat, 2 × 8 each leg | Sanchin stance hold with band over shoulders, 60–120 s |
| Hips | Glute bridge, 12 reps | Single-leg glute bridge, 8 each leg | Glute bridge hold 60 s, then cushion squeeze in sanchin dachi 30 s |
| Trunk | Front plank 20–30 s; side plank 15–20 s each | Front plank 45–60 s; side plank 30–40 s; bird dog 8 each side | Front plank 90 s; side plank 60 s; dead bug 10 each side |
| Back and shoulders | Band pull-apart, 15 reps | Band pull-apart 20; straight-arm pulldown 12 | Press-up plus 10; chi ishi block lever 8 each arm |
| Grip and forearms | Towel wring, 20 s each way | Dead hang 20–40 s; rice bucket 30 grabs | Nigiri game walk with loaded containers, 4 lengths; dead hang 45–60 s |

: **Table 24.2** Strength block exercises and progressions. Move to the next level within a phase if the current level feels easy with perfect form.

### 4. Sanchin practice (8–10 minutes)

| Phase | Repetitions | Breathing stage | Peak tension | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 2 | 3–4 | Posture and continuity; single-check practice |
| 2 | 3–4 | 3 | 4–6 (one session a week at 6) | Timing of breath, movement and tension |
| 3 | 4–5 | 3–4 | 5–7 (one session a week at 7) | Structure under testing; endurance late in the kata |

: **Table 24.3** Sanchin practice within Session A. Rest a minute between repetitions. Always stay below your spill point.

## Session B in Detail

### 1. Warm-up (3–5 minutes)

A shortened version of the warm-up.

### 2. Mobility routine (6–8 minutes)

The following routine addresses the three mobility areas checked in the assessment: the hips, the ankles and the upper back. Hold each stretch for thirty seconds, breathing slowly, without forcing.

1. **Kneeling hip-flexor stretch.** Kneel on one knee, the other foot flat in front. Tuck the pelvis and shift gently forward until you feel a stretch at the front of the rear hip. Thirty seconds each side.
2. **Ankle rocks.** In a half-kneeling position, front foot flat, gently move the front knee forward over the toes and back, keeping the heel down. Fifteen each side.
3. **Hip inward rotation.** Lie on your back, knees bent, feet wider than hips. Let both knees fall slowly inward towards each other, then return. Fifteen repetitions.
4. **Thoracic opener.** Lie on your side with knees bent and arms out in front, palms together. Open the top arm in an arc to the other side, rotating the upper back, then return. Eight each side.
5. **Doorway chest stretch.** Forearms on the doorframe at shoulder height, step gently through. Thirty seconds.
6. **Chin glide.** As in Drill 6.2. Ten repetitions.

![**Figure 24.3** The mobility routine. From left: kneeling hip-flexor stretch, ankle rocks, hip inward rotation, thoracic opener and doorway chest stretch.](figures/fig24_3.png)

### 3. Sanchin drills (6–8 minutes)

Choose one focus for each Session B, taken from your current fault list. Practise the relevant drill from this book. For example:

- a stance fault: Drill 4.1 (floor template) or Drill 5.1 (knee tracking);
- an upper-body fault: Drill 6.2 (upper body set) or Drill 13.1 (elbow pivot);
- a breathing fault: Drill 7.1 (one punch, one breath) or Drill 7.2 (belt breathing);
- a rhythm fault: Drill 9.3 (metronome kata);
- a turn fault: Drill 11.2 (turn ladder);
- the open-hand techniques: Drill 15.2 (circular block builder).

### 4. Calm kata (3 minutes)

One performance of Sanchin at Stage 2 breathing and light tension, with full attention. This is not a training load but a practice of quality. Finish with three breaths and a moment of stillness.

## Partner or Solo Testing

Once a week, ideally on Saturday, include testing. In Phase 1, use the standard round at Level 1. In Phase 2, move to Level 2 using the pressure ladder. In Phase 3, add the moving tests from Chapter 18. Record the results in your testing journal and use them to choose the focus for the following week's Session B.

If you train alone, use the Solo Round from Chapter 18.

## The Lighter Weeks

Weeks 4, 8 and 12 are lighter. In these weeks:

- perform Session A only twice, not three times;
- reduce the strength block to two rounds;
- reduce Sanchin repetitions by one and keep peak tension in the middle of your range;
- keep Session B and testing as normal.

Students who are keen often want to skip the lighter weeks. Please do not. The lighter week is when the gains of the previous three weeks consolidate. Students who skip them tend to stall or to pick up small injuries in the following phase.

## Reassessment

At the end of week 6 and week 12, repeat the full ten-part assessment from Chapter 22 and record the results in Appendix C. Compare them with your starting results.

Typical changes over twelve weeks, for students who follow the programme consistently, include longer stance holds, longer wall sits and planks, better balance and longer out-breaths. The tests that improve least are usually those linked to your oldest habits or your weaker side; they are your priorities for the next cycle.

Alongside the numbers, repeat your recordings from Chapter 18 and compare them with your earliest recordings. Look especially at the last minute of the kata. For most students, this is where the programme shows most clearly: the posture that used to collapse by the second turn now holds to the final bow.

::: test
**Test Yourself: Week 12 Review**

At the end of the programme, answer these questions in your training notebook:

1. Which three assessment results improved most?
2. Which result improved least, and what might explain it?
3. Comparing your week 1 and week 12 recordings, what is the most visible change in your Sanchin?
4. Which tests in the standard round now hold that did not before?
5. What will your priorities be for the next twelve weeks?
:::

## Adjusting the Programme

### If you are short of time

Do Session A twice a week instead of three times, and Session B once. Keep the hojo circuit and the Sanchin practice; shorten the strength block to one round. Even this reduced version will produce clear improvement if kept up for twelve weeks.

### If you are older or returning after a long break

Stay at Level 1 of each exercise for the whole of Phase 1, and move to Level 2 only when Level 1 feels genuinely easy. Keep Sanchin at Stages 2 and 3 and peak tension at five or below. Extend the programme to sixteen weeks by repeating Phase 1. Spend more time on mobility and balance, which become more important with age and respond very well to training.

### If you are recovering from injury

Speak to a physiotherapist or doctor before starting, and show them the exercises. Replace any exercise that aggravates your injury with one they recommend. Do not test the injured area. Progress more slowly than the programme suggests.

### If you are already well conditioned

Begin each exercise at Level 2. Add a fourth Session A in Phases 2 and 3 if you can recover from it. Use heavier traditional tools under your teacher's guidance. Include more Level 2 and Level 3 testing in Phase 3. Keep the lighter weeks.

### If you have a grading coming up

Time the programme so that a lighter week falls in the week before the grading. In the final two weeks, reduce the strength block and increase the number of calm, full-attention kata performances. Arrive at the grading rested, not exhausted.

::: fault
**Problem: "I have been following the programme for six weeks and my stance hold has not improved."**

*Check.* Look at your training log. Have you completed at least ninety per cent of the sessions? Have you progressed the leg exercises? Is your stance hold being stopped by your legs tiring, or by your posture breaking for another reason (shoulders, breath)?

*Cause.* Usually one of three things: inconsistency, lack of progression (doing the same wall sit time for six weeks), or a technique fault that wastes energy and makes the stance hold a test of something other than leg endurance.

*Correct.* If attendance has been patchy, recommit for another six weeks. If you have not progressed, increase hold times by ten per cent a week. If posture breaks before the legs tire, work on the relevant fault from Part Seven; the strength is there but is being wasted.

*Confirm.* Reassess at week 12.
:::

::: note
**Teacher's Note**

I have seen many students transform their Sanchin with a programme like this one, and the transformation is rarely the one they expected. They usually start because they want to be stronger. They usually finish having become more consistent. The structure of the programme, a little each day, a plan for each week, a check every few weeks, teaches the habit of steady practice. That habit, more than any particular exercise, is what makes Sanchin improve over years. The programme is a twelve-week container for a lifelong practice.
:::

::: keypoints
### Chapter Summary

- The programme has three four-week phases: Foundation, Build and Consolidate. The fourth week of each is lighter.
- Each week contains three Session A days (warm-up, hojo circuit, strength block, Sanchin practice) and two or three Session B days (warm-up, mobility, Sanchin drills, calm kata), plus dojo classes, testing and a rest day.
- Every strength exercise has three levels. Move up when the current level is easy with perfect form.
- Sanchin practice within the programme progresses from Stage 2 breathing and peak tension of 3–4 to Stages 3–4 and peak tension of 5–7, always below the spill point.
- Reassess with the ten-part assessment at weeks 6 and 12, and compare recordings.
- Adjust the programme for time, age, injury, existing fitness and gradings.
:::

::: practice
### Training Tasks

1. Plan your first week on the template in Appendix C, fitting Sessions A and B around your dojo classes.
2. Gather the equipment you need: resistance band, containers for the nigiri game, dowel and cord, a bucket of rice, a timer.
3. Start the programme on a Monday and record every session in your training log.
4. Mark weeks 4, 8 and 12 as lighter weeks in your diary now, so that you are not tempted to skip them.
5. At the end of week 12, complete the Week 12 Review and plan your next cycle.
