# The Return, the Retreat and the Close: Frames 25 to 48

::: chapter-intro
The second half of Sanchin brings the kata home. This chapter covers the last punch-and-block cycle facing the rear, the second turn back to the front, the retreat with the double spear-hand thrusts, the two circular blocks and the formal close. The closing techniques are different in character from everything that has gone before: more open, more circular and more flowing. They need a different kind of attention.
:::

By the time you reach Frame 25, you have performed six punch-and-block cycles and one turn. Your legs are working hard, your forearms are warm and your breath has settled into its rhythm. This is the point at which many students begin, without realising it, to coast. Their steps shorten, their breath quickens and their attention starts to drift towards the end of the kata.

Resist that. The second half of Sanchin contains some of its most demanding and most interesting movements. The second turn is harder than the first, because you are more tired. The double spear-hand asks you to hold structure while both arms extend at once. The circular blocks ask the hard body you have been building to become soft and round without losing its root. And the close asks you to finish the kata with the same quality you started it.

## Frames 25 to 27: The Cycle Facing the Rear

You begin in right sanchin dachi, right foot forward, facing the rear, with both arms in the double block, as at the end of Frame 24.

![**Figure 12.1** Frames 25 to 31. Facing the rear: left pulling hand, left punch and return. Then the second turn: the left foot slides across behind; the body pivots to face the front in left sanchin dachi with the double block; and a final right punch-and-block cycle facing the front.](figures/fig12_1.png)

### Frame 25 – Pulling hand (left)

**Action.** Draw the left fist to the left hip. **Breath.** In. **Checkpoints.** As Frame 7. Make sure the stance from Frame 24 has fully set.

### Frame 26 – Punch (left)

**Action.** Punch with the left fist to the centre line at solar-plexus height. **Breath.** Out. **Checkpoints.** As Frame 8. The rear foot, the left, presses into the floor; the rear heel stays down.

### Frame 27 – Return to block

**Action.** Return the left arm to the block and settle. **Breath.** Short exchange; end of out-breath. **Checkpoints.** As Frame 9. As before the first turn, take a moment here to settle completely.

## Frames 28 to 31: The Second Turn and the Last Cycle

### Frame 28 – Slide across

**Action.** Exactly as Frame 20: keeping the right foot planted, slide the left foot across behind the line of the right heel to a point shoulder-width to the far side of the right foot. The arms cross in front of the chest.

**Breath.** In, slowly.

**Checkpoints.** As Frame 20. Because you are now more tired, pay particular attention to head height and to the distance the left foot travels. Fatigue makes the slide shorter, and a short slide makes the pivot unstable.

### Frame 29 – Pivot and block, facing the front

**Action.** Pivot clockwise on both feet through 180 degrees to face the front again, arriving in left sanchin dachi. As the stance sets, open the arms into the double block.

**Breath.** Out, ending as the block and stance set.

**Checkpoints.** As Frame 21. Your eyes should arrive at the front gaze point you chose before the kata began. Check that you are still on the line of the floor pattern; if the turns have drifted sideways, you will notice it here.

### Frame 30 – Pulling hand and punch (right)

**Action.** Draw the right fist to the hip on an in-breath, then punch with it to the centre line on an out-breath. These two movements are combined in one frame because they are identical to Frames 13 and 14.

**Breath.** In, then out.

**Checkpoints.** As Frames 13 and 14.

### Frame 31 – Return to block

**Action.** Return the right arm to the block and settle. **Breath.** Short exchange; end of out-breath.

**Checkpoints.** As Frame 15. This is the last punch of the kata. It is also the last time the arms will be in the closed-fist double block for a while. Make it count.

::: note
**Teacher's Note**

I often ask students to treat the last punch of Sanchin as though it were the first. The temptation, at this point in the kata, is to think about what comes next, the spear-hands and the circular blocks, and to let the final cycle become a hurried formality. When you notice your mind running ahead, bring it back to the punch in front of you. That small act of attention is the inner battle of Chapter 3 in practice.
:::

## The Closing Techniques

The final part of Sanchin introduces two new techniques: the double spear-hand thrust, *morote nukite*, and the circular block, *mawashi uke*. Both are performed with open hands, and both move the body from the straight lines and closed fists of the first part of the kata towards curves and open palms.

Some teachers describe this transition as Sanchin briefly showing its soft side, a hint of the Ju that the student will meet more fully in Tensho. Others point out that the closing techniques are among the most practical in the kata, with clear applications in grappling and close-range defence, discussed in Chapter 26. Either way, they need a slightly different approach. The hands are open, which means the fingers must stay firm without being rigid. The movements are larger and more continuous, which means the breath is longer. And the circular block in particular asks for a smoothness that the earlier, more angular techniques do not.

Chapter 15 looks at both techniques in detail. Here we concentrate on their place in the kata.

## Frames 32 to 36: Stepping Back With Double Spear-Hands

You begin in left sanchin dachi, left foot forward, facing the front, with both arms in the double block.

![**Figure 12.2** Frames 32 to 36. The left foot steps back into right sanchin dachi as both hands open and draw to the hips; both hands thrust forward as a double spear-hand; the hands return to the hips; the right foot steps back into left sanchin dachi; and the double spear-hand is repeated.](figures/fig12_2.png)

### Frame 32 – Step back, hands to hips

**Action.** Slide the left foot backwards in a crescent, in past the right foot and out to its new position behind, so that you arrive in right sanchin dachi, right foot forward. As you step, open both hands and draw them back and down to the sides of the hips, palms facing up and slightly in, fingers pointing forward, elbows pointing back.

**Breath.** In, through the nose, for about three seconds.

**Checkpoints.** The step back is a mirror of the step forward: crescent path, head level, knees close as the feet pass. The open hands arrive at the hips together, at the same height, fingers together and firm. The shoulders stay down as the elbows draw back.

**Common errors.** Stepping back in a straight line; leaning forward as the foot goes back; letting the hands go limp; drawing the elbows out to the sides.

### Frame 33 – Double spear-hand (*morote nukite*)

**Action.** Thrust both open hands forward together, at about the height of your lower ribs or solar plexus, the hands roughly shoulder-width apart or slightly narrower, palms facing each other or slightly downward depending on your teacher. The fingers are straight and together, the thumbs tucked against the side of the hand. The elbows extend but do not lock.

**Breath.** Out, steady and audible, ending as the hands arrive and the tension peaks.

**Checkpoints.** The thrust is driven from the ground, through the back, as with the punch. Both arms arrive together, at the same height. The shoulders stay down and do not reach forward. The trunk stays upright.

**Common errors.** Hands at different heights; shoulders hunching forward; fingers bent or spread; thrusting too high, at the face, or too low, at the belt.

### Frame 34 – Return to hips

**Action.** Draw both open hands back to the hips, as in Frame 32.

**Breath.** In.

**Checkpoints.** The hands return along the same line they went out on. The back stays engaged.

### Frame 35 – Step back

**Action.** Slide the right foot backwards in a crescent into left sanchin dachi, left foot forward. The hands remain at the hips.

**Breath.** Continuation of the in-breath, or a short out-and-in, as your teacher prefers.

**Checkpoints.** As Frame 32.

### Frame 36 – Double spear-hand

**Action.** Repeat the double spear-hand thrust, as in Frame 33.

**Breath.** Out.

**Checkpoints.** As Frame 33.

::: fault
**Problem: "My spear-hands are uneven."**

*Check.* Film from the front and pause at the end of each thrust. Compare the height of the two hands and the angle of the two wrists.

*Cause.* One arm, almost always the non-dominant one, is weaker in its connection to the back, so it arrives lower or later. Sometimes the trunk is also twisting very slightly towards the dominant side.

*Correct.* Practise the double spear-hand in a fixed stance facing a mirror, with a strip of tape across the mirror at the target height. Ten slow repetitions, three times a week. Concentrate on drawing the shoulder blade down on the weaker side as the arm extends. If the trunk is twisting, practise with your back an inch from a wall so that you feel any rotation.

*Confirm.* Film again from the front.
:::

## Frames 37 to 42: Step Back and the Right Circular Block

You begin in left sanchin dachi, left foot forward, both open hands extended in the double spear-hand.

The circular block is the most complex movement in Sanchin. Both hands travel in circles, in opposite directions, before being pushed forward together into the "tiger's mouth" position, *tora guchi*: one hand above the other, palms forward, the wrists close, like the open jaws of a tiger. The block is named for the side of the upper hand. In the right circular block, the right hand finishes on top.

![**Figure 12.3** Frames 37 to 42, the right circular block. The left foot steps back into right sanchin dachi as the hands draw in; the hands begin to circle in opposite directions; they continue round; they draw back to the chest, palms forward; they push forward together into the tiger's-mouth position, right hand above, left hand below; the position settles.](figures/fig12_3.png)

### Frame 37 – Step back and draw in

**Action.** Slide the left foot back in a crescent into right sanchin dachi, right foot forward. At the same time, draw both open hands back towards the centre of the chest, the right hand slightly above the left.

**Breath.** In.

**Checkpoints.** As Frame 32 for the step. The hands are relaxed but firm, the shoulders down.

### Frame 38 – Circle begins

**Action.** Begin the circle. The right hand, palm facing forward, travels upwards and outwards in an arc, as if wiping a large window clockwise from your own point of view; the left hand travels downwards and outwards in the opposite direction. The two hands move together, at the same speed.

**Breath.** Continuation of the in-breath, slow and full.

**Checkpoints.** The circles are made from the shoulders and the back, not just the forearms. The elbows stay relatively low. The stance does not move.

### Frame 39 – Circle continues

**Action.** The hands continue round until they have exchanged positions in the circle: the right hand, which started on top, is now passing round the outside towards the bottom and back up; the left hand is passing round towards the top and down. In the version described here, each hand completes roughly one full circle and returns to its starting side, right above, left below.

**Breath.** End of the in-breath.

**Checkpoints.** The movement is continuous and smooth, with no corners. Both hands stay in front of the body, within the width of the shoulders, never sweeping out wide.

### Frame 40 – Draw to chest

**Action.** As the circles finish, draw both hands back towards the chest, the right hand above with its palm facing forward and fingers up, the left hand below with its palm facing forward and fingers down. The wrists are close together, the heels of the hands almost touching, forming the tiger's mouth close to the body.

**Breath.** A brief pause at the end of the in-breath, or the very beginning of the out-breath.

**Checkpoints.** The elbows are down and close to the body. The shoulders are down.

### Frame 41 – Push (*tora guchi*)

**Action.** Push both hands forward together, keeping the tiger's mouth shape, until the arms are almost straight. The right hand, on top, finishes at about chin or upper-chest height; the left hand, below, at about the level of the lower abdomen. The palms face forward, the heels of the hands lead.

**Breath.** Out, long, steady and audible, ending as the push arrives and the tension peaks.

**Checkpoints.** The push is driven from the legs and back, with the whole body behind it. Both hands arrive together. The shoulders stay down. The trunk stays upright, not leaning into the push.

**Common errors.** Pushing with the arms alone; leaning forward; hands separating as they travel; elbows locking.

### Frame 42 – Settle

**Action.** Hold the tiger's mouth briefly as the tension peaks and eases.

**Breath.** End of the out-breath; brief pause.

**Checkpoints.** The whole structure is set: feet gripping, knees braced, pelvis tucked, back wide, shoulders down, face calm.

## Frames 43 to 46: Step Back and the Left Circular Block

These frames mirror Frames 37 to 42. You step back with the right foot into left sanchin dachi and perform the circular block with the left hand finishing on top.

![**Figure 12.4** Frames 43 to 48. The right foot steps back into left sanchin dachi and the hands circle in the opposite direction; the left circular block pushes forward with the left hand above; the front foot draws back to musubi dachi as the hands return to the ready position; attention and the final bow.](figures/fig12_4.png)

### Frame 43 – Step back and draw in

**Action.** Slide the right foot back in a crescent into left sanchin dachi, left foot forward, drawing both hands towards the chest, left hand slightly above. **Breath.** In.

### Frame 44 – Circle

**Action.** The hands circle in the opposite direction to the right block: the left hand travels up and outwards anticlockwise from your own point of view, the right hand down and outwards. Each completes roughly one circle and draws back to the chest, left hand above, palm forward. Frames 38 to 40 are combined here. **Breath.** Continuation and end of the in-breath.

### Frame 45 – Push

**Action.** Push forward into the tiger's mouth, left hand on top, right hand below. **Breath.** Out, long and steady.

### Frame 46 – Settle

**Action.** Hold briefly as the tension peaks and eases. **Breath.** End of the out-breath.

**Checkpoints for Frames 43 to 46.** As for Frames 37 to 42, mirrored. Most students have a better side for the circular block. Film both and compare.

::: drill
**Drill 12.1 – Circles Without Feet**

*What to do:* Stand in a parallel stance, feet shoulder-width apart, knees soft. Perform the hand circles of the right circular block slowly, ten times, without the push, letting the breath come in as the hands circle and go out as they draw to the chest. Then perform ten circles of the left circular block. Then perform five complete right and five complete left circular blocks, including the push, still in a parallel stance.

*Why it matters:* The circular block is complex enough that most students lose their stance when they first try it. Removing the stance from the equation lets you learn the hands first. Once the circles are smooth, return them to sanchin dachi.
:::

## Frames 47 and 48: The Close

### Frame 47 – Return to ready

**Action.** Draw the front foot, the left, back to the right foot, heels together, toes turned out into musubi dachi. At the same time, bring both hands down and in to cross, open, in front of the lower abdomen, left over right, as in Frame 3.

**Breath.** In as the foot draws back; out as the hands settle.

**Checkpoints.** The feet arrive exactly together, heels touching. The hands are in the same yoi position as at the start. Where are you on the floor? You should be close to the spot where you began. If you have drifted forward or back by more than a step, one or more of your steps has been consistently long or short.

**Common errors.** Rushing the close; dropping the tension and posture completely the moment the last technique is done; looking down at the feet.

### Frame 48 – Attention and bow

**Action.** Lower the hands to the sides of the thighs, return to attention, then bow as in Frame 2 and return to upright.

**Breath.** Natural.

**Checkpoints.** The kata finishes as it began: calm, upright, steady eyes. Remain still for a breath or two after the bow before moving away. Many teachers call this moment of continued awareness *zanshin*.

::: test
**Test Yourself: Where Did You Finish?**

Mark your starting position on the floor with a small piece of tape at your heels before you begin. Perform the whole kata. At Frame 47, before you move, look down at the tape.

- Within a few centimetres: your steps are consistent. Well done.
- More than a step forward: your forward steps are longer than your backward steps, or your turns are drifting forward.
- More than a step back: your backward steps are longer, which often means you are rushing the retreat.
- To one side: one or both turns are drifting sideways. Revisit Drill 11.2.
:::

## Putting the Kata Together

Learning the kata in frames is the right way to begin, but eventually the frames must disappear. Sanchin is one continuous movement, not forty-eight separate ones, and a performance that still looks like a sequence of frames has not yet become a kata.

The best way to make that transition is gradually. Once you can perform each chapter's frames comfortably, link them: Frames 1 to 24 as one unit, then Frames 25 to 48 as another, then the whole. Use the rhythm work of Chapter 9 to keep the transitions smooth, especially at the joins between the sections where you previously stopped.

Then, for a while, stop reading. Perform the kata from memory, several times a week, and use the book only to check specific frames when something feels wrong or when your teacher makes a correction. The frames are scaffolding. When the building can stand, the scaffolding comes down.

::: keypoints
### Chapter Summary

- The second half of Sanchin is as demanding as the first, and fatigue makes it easy to coast. Treat the last punch as though it were the first.
- The second turn mirrors the first. Check on arrival that you are still on the line of the floor pattern.
- The closing techniques use open hands and curved, continuous movements, giving a glimpse of the soft side of Goju-Ryu.
- The double spear-hand is performed twice, stepping back, with both hands extending together from the hips at solar-plexus height.
- The circular block is performed on the right and then the left, stepping back each time. The hands circle in opposite directions and push forward together into the tiger's-mouth position.
- The close returns you to musubi dachi and the ready position, then attention and a bow. You should finish close to where you started.
- Once the frames are learned, link them into larger units and eventually into one continuous kata.
:::

::: practice
### Training Tasks

1. Practise Frames 25 to 31 as a unit, three times, focusing on the second turn.
2. Practise the double spear-hand with the mirror and tape exercise from the problem box, ten repetitions, three times a week.
3. Practise Drill 12.1, circles without feet, until the circles are smooth on both sides, then return them to sanchin dachi.
4. Carry out the Where Did You Finish? test three times and record the results.
5. Perform the whole kata, Frames 1 to 48, once a day for a week at Stage 2 breathing and light tension, concentrating only on continuity.
6. Repeat Drill 1.1 from Chapter 1: record your whole Sanchin from the side and the front. Compare it with your baseline recording. Write down three things that have improved and three that still need work.
:::
