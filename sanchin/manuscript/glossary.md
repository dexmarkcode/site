# Glossary

Japanese terms are given in the romanised forms most commonly used in English-language Goju-Ryu teaching. Spellings and meanings vary slightly between associations.

Bubishi
:   A Chinese text on martial arts and medicine that circulated among Okinawan karateka. The name Goju-Ryu was taken from a line in it.

Bunkai
:   Analysis of a kata; the study of its practical applications.

Chi ishi
:   "Strength stone". A weight on the end of a wooden handle, used in hojo undo to strengthen the grip, wrist and shoulder.

Chudan
:   Middle level; the area of the body between the belt and the shoulders.

Chudan uke
:   Middle-level block. In Sanchin, the block with the forearm upright and the fist at shoulder height. Also called uchi uke in some associations.

Dachi
:   Stance.

Embusen
:   The floor pattern of a kata; the path traced by the feet.

Fudoshin
:   "Immovable mind". A mind not shaken by fear, surprise or pain.

Goju-Ryu
:   "Hard-soft school". The style of karate founded by Chojun Miyagi.

Heishu kata
:   "Closed-hand" kata; in Goju-Ryu, usually Sanchin and Tensho, which develop the body and breath.

Hikite
:   Pulling hand. The hand drawn back to the side of the body, palm up.

Hojo undo
:   Supplementary exercise; traditional strength training with simple tools.

Ibuki
:   The strong, audible, "hard" breath of Sanchin.

Ishi sashi
:   Stone or iron weights shaped like padlocks, used in hojo undo.

Jari bako
:   A box or bucket of gravel, beans or rice, used to strengthen the fingers.

Kaishu kata
:   "Open-hand" kata; the kata of Goju-Ryu other than Sanchin and Tensho.

Kakie
:   A partner practice in which two people push and yield with their wrists or forearms in contact. Often compared to Chinese pushing hands.

Kata
:   A prearranged sequence of movements practised alone.

Kongoken
:   A large, heavy oval iron ring used in hojo undo.

Makiage kigu
:   A wrist roller: a bar with a cord and weight, wound up and down.

Makiwara
:   A padded striking post.

Mawashi uke
:   Circular block; the two-handed block that closes Sanchin.

Morote
:   Two-handed; both hands together.

Morote chudan uke
:   Double middle-level block.

Morote nukite
:   Double spear-hand thrust.

Musubi dachi
:   Attention stance with the heels together and the toes turned out.

Naha-te
:   The karate of Naha, taught by Kanryo Higaonna; the forerunner of Goju-Ryu.

Nigiri game
:   Gripping jars, held by the rim and carried in hojo undo.

Nogare
:   The soft, quiet breath.

Nukite
:   Spear-hand; a strike with the straight fingertips.

Rei
:   Bow.

Rokkishu
:   "Six hand techniques"; a set of open-hand techniques in the Bubishi, said to have inspired Tensho.

Sanchin
:   "Three battles" or "three conflicts". The foundational kata of Goju-Ryu.

Sanchin dachi
:   The stance of Sanchin: short, shoulder-width, front foot turned in, knees drawn in.

Seiken
:   The fore-fist; the first two knuckles of the closed fist.

Shiko dachi
:   A wide straddle stance with the toes turned out and the knees bent.

Shime
:   Tightening or closing. Both the closed, connected state of the body in Sanchin and the practice of testing it by hand.

Shotei
:   Palm heel.

Tanden
:   The area of the lower abdomen regarded as the body's centre. Also called hara.

Tensho
:   "Rotating palms". The soft partner kata to Sanchin, composed by Chojun Miyagi.

Tora guchi
:   "Tiger's mouth". The two-handed position at the end of the circular block, one palm above the other.

Tsuki
:   Punch or thrust.

Uke
:   Block; also the person receiving a technique.

Yoi
:   Ready; the ready position.

Zanshin
:   "Remaining mind". Continued alert awareness after a technique or kata has finished.
