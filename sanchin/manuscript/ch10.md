# The Opening: Frames 1 to 10

::: chapter-intro
This is the first of three chapters that take you through the whole of Sanchin, frame by frame. It begins by setting out exactly which version of the kata is described and where other lineages commonly differ, explains how to read the frame diagrams and shows the floor pattern of the whole form. It then covers the first ten frames: the formal opening, the first step into sanchin dachi, the double block and the first complete punch-and-block cycle.
:::

Everything you have learned so far now comes together. The stance from Part Two, the breath and tension from Part Three, the principles from Part One: all of them are put to work in the movements of the kata itself.

These three chapters are designed to be used on your feet. Read a frame, put the book down, perform the frame, and then read it again. You will notice details on the second reading that meant nothing on the first. Do not try to take in all forty-eight frames at once. A sensible pace for a student learning the kata for the first time is a few frames per session, with plenty of repetition, over several weeks.

If you already know Sanchin, these chapters will still repay close reading. Experienced students tend to lose small details over the years: the exact path of a hand, the precise moment of a breath. Working through the frames slowly is one of the best ways to find out which details have drifted.

## The Version Described

As explained in Chapter 2, Sanchin is practised in several versions. This book describes the closed-fist version with turns, as broadly taught in the Okinawan lines descended from Chojun Miyagi. The sequence, in summary, is as follows.

1. A formal opening: attention, bow and ready position.
2. A step forward into right sanchin dachi with a double middle block.
3. Three punch-and-block cycles moving forward: one in place, then one with each of two forward steps.
4. A turn to face the rear, followed by a punch-and-block cycle, a step forward and another cycle.
5. A second turn to face the front again, followed by a punch-and-block cycle.
6. Two steps backward, each with a double spear-hand thrust.
7. A step back and a circular block on the right, then a step back and a circular block on the left.
8. A formal close: return to the ready position, attention and bow.

Table 10.1 lists the points at which other versions most commonly differ. If your own dojo does any of these differently, mark the frames concerned in pencil and follow your teacher. Nearly everything else in these chapters, the stance checks, the breath timing, the common errors, will still apply.

| Element | This book | Common alternatives |
|---|---|---|
| Hands | Closed fists | Open hands (older versions; Uechi-Ryu) |
| Turns | Two turns of 180° | No turns; one turn; turns at different points |
| Steps forward before the first turn | Three (including the opening step) | Two, four or more |
| Breath on return to block | Short in, short out | A single breath; breath only on the punch |
| Double spear-hand | Twice, stepping back | Once; performed stepping forward; omitted |
| Circular block | Right then left, stepping back | One only; performed in place |
| Breath sound | Steady "haa" through partly open mouth | Closed-mouth nasal breath; stronger ibuki throughout |

## How to Read the Frames

Each frame in these chapters is a single, clearly defined moment in the kata. Some frames are positions you pass through; others are positions where you settle briefly at the peak of a technique. For each frame you will find:

- **A diagram.** Frames are grouped into strips of three to five, drawn mostly from a three-quarter view so that both arms and both legs are visible. A dashed grey figure shows the previous position where that helps. Arrows show the direction of movement. The black circle at the top left of each figure gives the frame number, and the label at the top right gives the breath: IN, OUT, or a short exchange.
- **The action.** What moves, where and how.
- **The breath.** In, out, or an exchange, with the approximate timing from Chapter 9.
- **Checkpoints.** The specific points a teacher would look for at that frame.
- **Common errors.** The faults that most often appear at that frame, with a pointer to the relevant correction elsewhere in the book.

Directions are given relative to the way you are facing at the start of the kata. "Front" means the direction you face at the bow; "rear" means the opposite direction.

## The Floor Pattern

The floor pattern of a kata, its *embusen*, is the path the feet trace on the floor. Sanchin's floor pattern is short and simple: a straight line along which you move forward, turn, move again, turn back and finally retreat. It needs a space roughly three metres long and a metre and a half wide.

Figure 10.1 shows the whole pattern from above, with the frame numbers at which each foot position is reached. It is worth spending a few minutes with this figure before you start, so that you have a picture of where the kata goes.

![**Figure 10.1** The floor pattern of Sanchin as described in this book, seen from above. Numbers show the frames at which the main positions are reached. The kata advances three steps along the line, turns to the rear, advances one step, turns to the front again, retreats two steps with spear-hands and two more with circular blocks, and closes close to where it began.](figures/fig10_1.png)

::: note
**Teacher's Note**

Before you perform the kata, look along your floor pattern and pick a fixed point at eye height at each end of the room. These are your gaze points for the front and the rear. Having them chosen in advance stops your eyes wandering at the turns, which is one of the most common moments for the posture to break.
:::

## Frames 1 to 3: Attention, Bow and Ready

The opening of Sanchin is short, but it is not a formality. It sets the posture, the breath and the state of mind for everything that follows. Many teachers say that they can tell how a student's Sanchin will go from the way they stand at attention.

![**Figure 10.2** Frames 1 to 3. Attention in musubi dachi, the bow, and the ready position with open hands crossed in front of the lower abdomen.](figures/fig10_2.png)

### Frame 1 – Attention (*musubi dachi*)

**Action.** Stand with your heels together and your toes turned out at roughly forty-five degrees to each other, forming a V. This is *musubi dachi*. Your arms hang naturally at your sides, hands open, fingers together, resting lightly against the outsides of your thighs. Your knees are straight but not locked.

**Breath.** Natural breathing through the nose.

**Checkpoints.** The weight is even across both feet. The pelvis is in a neutral position, neither tucked nor tilted. The chin is slightly drawn in, the crown of the head lifting. The eyes look straight ahead at your gaze point.

**Common errors.** Fidgeting or adjusting the belt; looking down; feet too far apart; knees locked back. The posture should be calm and alert, not stiff.

### Frame 2 – The bow (*rei*)

**Action.** Bow from the hips by about fifteen to thirty degrees, keeping your back straight and your neck in line with your spine. Your hands remain at your sides. Pause briefly at the bottom of the bow, then return smoothly to upright.

**Breath.** Most teachers ask students to breathe out gently during the bow and in as they return, or simply to breathe naturally. Follow your teacher.

**Checkpoints.** The back stays long; the bow comes from the hips, not from rounding the upper back. Many teachers ask that the eyes remain forward, or at least that the gaze lowers only slightly, rather than dropping to the floor.

**Common errors.** Bowing only with the head; bowing too deeply or too quickly; letting the hands swing forward.

### Frame 3 – Ready (*yoi*)

**Action.** Bring both open hands up and forward to cross in front of your lower abdomen, at about the height of the belt knot, with the left hand over the right and the palms facing your body. The hands are relaxed but the fingers are together. At the same time, settle your weight very slightly and let your knees soften.

**Breath.** Breathe in slowly through the nose as the hands come together; breathe out gently as you settle. Some lines use this moment for a deeper, deliberate breath that sets the rhythm for the kata.

**Checkpoints.** The shoulders stay down as the hands rise. The elbows hang naturally, slightly away from the body. The hands are about a fist's width in front of the abdomen, not pressed against it. The gaze is steady.

**Common errors.** Lifting the shoulders as the hands come up; crossing the hands too high, at the chest; tensing the hands. The yoi position should feel quiet and gathered, like a held breath before music begins.

::: drill
**Drill 10.1 – Opening Ten Times**

Perform Frames 1 to 3 ten times in a row, slowly, with full attention. After each repetition, return to attention and pause for two breaths. Aim for each opening to be identical: the same foot position, the same depth of bow, the same height of hands. This trains the habit of beginning the kata in the same settled state every time.
:::

## Frames 4 to 6: The First Step and Double Block

These three frames take you from the ready position into sanchin dachi and set the arms in the double middle block, *morote chudan uke*, the position in which much of the kata is performed.

![**Figure 10.3** Frames 4 to 6. The right foot steps forward in a crescent into right sanchin dachi as the arms cross in front of the chest; the arms then open into the double middle block; the stance and block settle at peak tension.](figures/fig10_3.png)

### Frame 4 – Step and cross

**Action.** From the ready position, slide your right foot forward in a crescent: first in towards the left foot, then out and forward to its position in right sanchin dachi, front heel roughly level with the left toes, front toes turned in. As you step, your left foot pivots slightly on the heel so that its toes point forward or slightly in. At the same time, your hands close into fists and rise to cross in front of your chest, at about the height of the lower edge of the breastbone, right arm outside the left, with the backs of the wrists facing forward.

**Breath.** In, through the nose, for about three seconds.

**Checkpoints.** The head stays level throughout the step. The knees stay close as the right foot passes the left. The crossed forearms are a fist's width from the chest; the elbows stay down. The right foot arrives already turned in and grips as it lands.

**Common errors.** Stepping in a straight line instead of a crescent; lifting the foot; rising during the step; crossing the arms with raised shoulders. See Chapter 4 for the crescent step and Chapter 6 for the shoulders.

### Frame 5 – Double middle block (*morote chudan uke*)

**Action.** Open the crossed arms outwards and upwards into the block position. Each forearm travels outward and rises until it is roughly vertical, the fist at about shoulder height, a little in front of the shoulder and slightly outside it. The elbows remain down, a fist's width from the sides of the body. The inside of each forearm faces forward and slightly inward. The fists are closed firmly, the knuckles facing up and slightly out.

**Breath.** Out, through the mouth, for about five seconds, with a steady, audible sound.

**Checkpoints.** The block is driven by the back, not the shoulders: the shoulder blades draw down as the arms open, and the latissimus muscles engage. The tension builds from the ground up as described in Chapter 8 and peaks as the arms arrive. The pelvis stays tucked. The face stays calm.

**Common errors.** Fists too high (above the head) or too wide (outside the shoulders); elbows flaring out; shoulders rising; breath ending before the arms arrive. Chapter 13 deals with the block in detail.

### Frame 6 – Settle

**Action.** At the end of the block, hold the position for a moment as the tension reaches its peak and begins to ease back to the baseline. Nothing moves visibly, but internally the body checks itself: feet gripping, knees braced, pelvis tucked, back wide, shoulders down, eyes forward.

**Breath.** The last part of the out-breath, then a very brief natural pause.

**Checkpoints.** This is the moment at which a teacher will often first test the stance, pressing on the shoulders or the arms. The structure should hold without the student having to tense further.

**Common errors.** Freezing and holding the breath; letting the tension drop away completely so that the stance sags; adjusting the feet. If you need to adjust your feet here, your step was not accurate; go back to Drill 4.1.

::: fault
**Problem: "By the time I reach the double block, my stance has already gone wrong."**

*Check.* Film Frames 3 to 6 from the front and from the side. Look at the foot positions at Frame 5 and compare them with your floor template from Drill 4.1.

*Cause.* The first step is taken from a narrow, heels-together position, which is different from the steps later in the kata. Many students misjudge the width on this first step, ending too narrow, or turn the front foot in too late.

*Correct.* Practise Frame 4 on its own, stepping from musubi dachi onto your floor template, twenty times. Concentrate on placing the right foot at the correct width on the first attempt and turning the toes in as the foot lands, not afterwards.

*Confirm.* Film again and compare.
:::

## Frames 7 to 10: The First Punch-and-Block Cycle

The punch-and-block cycle is the basic unit of Sanchin. It is repeated, with small variations, nine times in the version described here. Getting it right in this first, stationary cycle makes all the later ones easier.

In right sanchin dachi, with the right foot forward, the first punch is made with the left hand. In general, throughout the kata, the punch is made with the hand on the same side as the rear foot. This keeps the hips square and allows the rear leg to drive the punch.

![**Figure 10.4** Frames 7 to 10, the first punch-and-block cycle, in right sanchin dachi. The left fist draws back to the hip; the left fist punches to the centre line at solar-plexus height while the right arm holds the block; the left arm returns to the block; the position settles.](figures/fig10_4.png)

### Frame 7 – Pulling hand (*hikite*)

**Action.** Open the left fist slightly and draw it back and down in a smooth arc to the left side of your body, closing it again as it arrives at the hip, palm facing up. The fist sits at the side of the body, just above the hip bone, the elbow pointing straight back. The right arm stays in the block position, unmoved.

**Breath.** In, through the nose, for about three seconds.

**Checkpoints.** The left elbow draws straight back, close to the body, not flaring out. The left shoulder does not roll forward or rise as the arm moves. The right block does not drift. The trunk does not twist.

**Common errors.** Pulling the fist back too far, behind the body, which rolls the shoulder back; lifting the left shoulder; letting the right block drop. Some teachers keep the fist closed throughout the pull; follow your teacher.

### Frame 8 – Punch (*seiken chudan tsuki*)

**Action.** Drive the left fist slowly forward along a straight line to the centre line, at the height of your own solar plexus. The fist rotates during the last third of the movement, so that it arrives palm down. The elbow straightens but does not lock. The right arm remains in the block.

**Breath.** Out, through the mouth, for about five seconds, steady and audible, ending as the punch arrives and the tension peaks.

**Checkpoints.** The punch is driven from the ground: the tension sequence of Chapter 8, from the feet to the fist. The left shoulder stays down and back, connected to the back; it does not reach forward. The trunk stays square. The fist arrives on the centre line, not across it. The face stays calm.

**Common errors.** Reaching with the shoulder; punching across the centre line; rotating the fist too early; the elbow flaring out sideways; leaning forward. See Chapter 14 for the punch in detail.

### Frame 9 – Return to block

**Action.** From the punch, bring the left arm back into the block position. The fist travels back and slightly outward, the elbow bending and dropping, until the forearm is upright and the fist is at shoulder height in front of the left shoulder, matching the right.

**Breath.** A short exchange: a quick in-breath through the nose as the arm begins to return, and a short out-breath as it settles into the block. Some teachers use a single breath here; follow your teacher.

**Checkpoints.** The arm returns under control, with the back still engaged. The elbow comes down and in, not out. The two blocks end symmetrical.

**Common errors.** Letting the arm drop loosely before re-forming the block; lifting the elbow out to the side on the way back; losing the stance as the arm returns. See Chapter 9 on transitions.

### Frame 10 – Settle

**Action.** Hold the double block briefly as the tension peaks and eases, exactly as in Frame 6.

**Breath.** End of the out-breath, brief pause.

**Checkpoints.** The same as Frame 6. This is the position from which the first forward step will be taken.

**Common errors.** The same as Frame 6.

::: drill
**Drill 10.2 – The Stationary Cycle**

*What to do:* Step into right sanchin dachi and set the double block. Perform Frames 7 to 10 with the left hand, ten times in a row, at your current breathing stage. Then change to left sanchin dachi and perform the same cycle with the right hand, ten times.

*What to look for:* Film one set from the side. Check that the punch arrives on the centre line at solar-plexus height, that the shoulder stays down, and that the breath ends as the punch arrives.

*Why it matters:* The stationary cycle is the backbone of the kata. Every minute spent perfecting it pays off nine times over in the full form.
:::

## Practising the Opening

The ten frames of this chapter make a complete practice unit on their own. You can perform them as a short exercise when time is limited, and they contain most of the key elements of the kata: the stance, the step, the block, the pulling hand, the punch, the return and the breath for each.

A useful way to build the opening is in three stages over two or three weeks.

**Stage A: shape.** Perform Frames 1 to 10 with natural breathing and light tension, concentrating on the positions only. Use the floor template and the mirror. Ten repetitions per session.

**Stage B: breath.** Add the breath for each frame at your current stage from Chapter 7. Keep the tension light. Concentrate on making the breath and the movement start and finish together.

**Stage C: tension.** Add tension, building through each out-breath to a peak at the end of the technique, at your current level from Chapter 8. Keep the face and shoulders soft.

Once Stage C feels settled, move on to the frames in Chapter 11.

::: test
**Test Yourself: The Opening Checklist**

Perform Frames 1 to 10 once, then answer yes or no:

1. Did your head stay level during the first step?
2. Did your right foot land already turned in, without adjustment?
3. Were both fists at shoulder height in the double block, with the elbows down?
4. Did your left elbow draw straight back on the pulling hand?
5. Did the punch arrive on the centre line at solar-plexus height?
6. Did your left shoulder stay down during the punch?
7. Did each out-breath finish as the technique arrived?
8. Did your face stay calm throughout?

Each "no" is a frame to practise on its own before moving on.
:::
