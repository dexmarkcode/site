# The Testing Map

::: chapter-intro
This chapter is a working reference for testing Sanchin, point by point. It sets out seventeen test points across the body, from the crown of the head to the calves, with the direction of force, the appropriate level, what each test checks, what failure looks like and where in this book to find the correction. It closes with a standard testing round that an instructor or training partner can run in about three minutes.
:::

Most students meet testing in fragments. One evening the teacher presses their shoulders; a week later someone slaps their thighs; months later a senior student pushes their fist at the end of a punch. Each test teaches something, but the student rarely sees how the tests fit together or what each one is designed to reveal.

This chapter puts the tests in order. Think of it as a map of the body, marked with the places where a teacher's hands go and the questions they are asking at each place. The tests are grouped by region: the head and upper body, the arms, the trunk, the hips and the legs. Each is described in the same way, so that you can find what you need quickly.

Before you use this chapter with a partner, read Chapter 16 again, particularly the rules and the no-go areas. Every test in this chapter can be performed at Level 1 or Level 2; a few can also be performed at Level 3, and these are marked. None of them should be performed at Level 4 except under the supervision of an experienced instructor.

## How the Tests Are Described

Each test point is set out as follows.

- **Where.** The exact place the tester's hands go.
- **Direction.** The direction of the pressure or contact.
- **Levels.** Which levels the test is suitable for.
- **Checks.** What the test reveals about the student's structure.
- **Failure looks like.** What the tester sees or feels when the structure does not hold.
- **Correction.** Where to look in this book.

The numbers of the test points match the numbers on Figures 17.1 and 17.2.

## The Head and Upper Body

### Test 1 – Crown alignment

**Where.** The tester rests the fingertips of one hand lightly on the crown of the student's head.

**Direction.** No pressure. The fingertips simply rest there, or press very lightly downward.

**Levels.** 1 only.

**Checks.** The lengthening of the spine and the steadiness of the head. The student should feel a gentle sense of growing up into the fingertips. The tester feels whether the head rises and falls with the breath or the steps.

**Failure looks like.** The head bobs during steps or turns; the head sinks on the out-breath as the student "squashes" down into the tension.

**Correction.** Chapter 6, The Long Spine; Chapter 4, head-bobbing problem.

### Test 2 – Shoulder press

**Where.** The tester stands behind the student and places both palms on the tops of the student's shoulders, close to the neck but not on it, on the thick muscle there.

**Direction.** Straight down.

**Levels.** 1, 2. A light Level 3 pat is sometimes used on the same muscles.

**Checks.** Whether the shoulder blades are drawn down and the tops of the shoulders relaxed, and whether the pressure is transmitted down through the trunk and legs into the floor.

**Failure looks like.** The shoulders are pushed down easily, which means they were raised to begin with; the student pushes upwards against the tester, which means they are bracing with the upper trapezius rather than holding with the back; the knees bend or the stance sinks under the pressure, which means the legs are not braced.

**Correction.** Chapter 6, The Shoulders; Chapter 5, the bend of the knees.

### Test 3 – Shoulder blades

**Where.** The tester places the flats of the fingers on the student's back, over the lower inside edges of the shoulder blades.

**Direction.** Light pressure inwards and upwards, as if trying to slide the shoulder blades up the back.

**Levels.** 1, 2.

**Checks.** Whether the shoulder blades are drawn down and flat against the ribcage.

**Failure looks like.** The shoulder blades slide up easily or "wing" away from the ribs, with an edge the tester can get their fingers under.

**Correction.** Chapter 6, Drawing the shoulders down; Drill 13.3.

### Test 4 – Latissimus

**Where.** The tester places the palms on the sides of the student's back, just below the armpits, over the broad muscles there.

**Direction.** Light inward pressure; at Level 3, open-hand pats.

**Levels.** 1, 2, 3.

**Checks.** Whether the latissimus muscles are engaged, connecting the arms to the trunk.

**Failure looks like.** The muscle is soft under the hand; the tester's hands sink in; at Level 3 the pat produces a dull, soft sound rather than a firm one.

**Correction.** Chapter 6, Connecting the arms to the trunk.

## The Arms

### Test 5 – Fist push down

**Where.** The tester stands in front of the student and places a palm on the top of the student's fist in the block position.

**Direction.** Straight down.

**Levels.** 1, 2.

**Checks.** Whether the block is braced by the back and holds its angle.

**Failure looks like.** The forearm folds down towards the shoulder; the shoulder rises; the elbow flares out.

**Correction.** Chapter 13, Connecting the block to the back.

### Test 6 – Forearm push out and in

**Where.** The tester places a palm on the inside of the student's wrist (to push outwards) or the outside (to push inwards).

**Direction.** Horizontally outwards, then horizontally inwards.

**Levels.** 1, 2.

**Checks.** Whether the block holds its line under sideways pressure.

**Failure looks like.** The forearm swings out from the shoulder, or collapses in towards the centre; the trunk rotates.

**Correction.** Chapter 13, Test Yourself: The Three-Push Block Check; Drill 13.3.

### Test 7 – Elbow lift

**Where.** The tester places a palm under the student's elbow in the block position.

**Direction.** Straight up.

**Levels.** 1, 2.

**Checks.** Whether the elbow is held down by the back.

**Failure looks like.** The elbow rises easily, often taking the shoulder with it.

**Correction.** Chapter 6, The position of the elbows; Test Yourself: The Elbow Push.

### Test 8 – Punch push-back

**Where.** At the end of a punch, the tester places a palm flat against the front of the student's fist.

**Direction.** Straight back along the line of the forearm.

**Levels.** 1, 2.

**Checks.** The whole chain of the punch: fist alignment, arm brace, shoulder connection, trunk, pelvis, rear leg.

**Failure looks like.** The elbow bends (arm not braced); the shoulder is pushed back or up (arm not connected); the body rocks back (stance not rooted, rear leg not pressing); the wrist bends (fist not aligned).

**Correction.** Chapter 14, Test Yourself: The Push-Back.

### Test 9 – Upper arms

**Where.** The tester places a hand on the back of the student's upper arm, over the triceps, with the arm in the block or at the end of a punch.

**Direction.** Light pressure; at Level 3, open-hand pats.

**Levels.** 1, 2, 3.

**Checks.** Whether the arm muscles are engaged, particularly at the peak of a technique.

**Failure looks like.** The muscle is soft at the moment it should be firm; at Level 3, the student flinches or the arm moves.

**Correction.** Chapter 8, Sequencing tension.

## The Trunk

### Test 10 – Abdomen

**Where.** The tester places the flat of one palm on the student's abdomen, just below the navel.

**Direction.** Steady inward pressure, applied only during the out-breath and released before the in-breath. Never a strike.

**Levels.** 1, 2 only.

**Checks.** Whether the lower abdomen is active and the trunk braced around the breath.

**Failure looks like.** The tester's hand sinks in easily; the student's breath catches; the student leans back.

**Correction.** Chapter 7, The out-breath; Chapter 3, Centring.

::: note
**Teacher's Note**

Some lineages test the abdomen with strikes, and you may see this done in demonstrations. I do not recommend it, except between very experienced practitioners under close supervision, and I never do it with students below black-belt level. The organs behind the abdominal wall are vulnerable, the timing of the breath has to be exact, and the risk is not worth the small additional training benefit over steady pressure. A firm palm on the out-breath tells you almost everything a strike would.
:::

### Test 11 – Sides of the trunk

**Where.** The tester places the palms on the sides of the student's trunk, between the bottom of the ribs and the top of the hip bones, on the muscles of the waist.

**Direction.** Inward pressure; at Level 3, light open-hand pats.

**Levels.** 1, 2, 3 (light).

**Checks.** Whether the breath expands the sides of the trunk and whether the side muscles are engaged.

**Failure looks like.** No sideways expansion on the in-breath; softness on the out-breath.

**Correction.** Chapter 7, Drill 7.2 Belt Breathing.

### Test 12 – Lower back

**Where.** The tester places the flat of a hand on the student's lower back, just above the belt, across the spine.

**Direction.** No pressure, or very light. This is a feeling test.

**Levels.** 1 only. Never strike this area.

**Checks.** The position of the pelvis and the expansion of the back on the in-breath.

**Failure looks like.** The lower back hollows away from the hand (pelvis tilted forward); the back pushes hard into the hand (over-tucked); no widening on the in-breath.

**Correction.** Chapter 5, The pelvis; Test Yourself: The Wall Check.

## The Hips and Legs

### Test 13 – Hip push

**Where.** The tester stands beside the student and places a palm on the side of the student's hip at belt height.

**Direction.** Horizontally sideways, towards the student's other side.

**Levels.** 1, 2.

**Checks.** The lateral stability of the stance and the bracing of both legs.

**Failure looks like.** The hips shift sideways; the upper body tilts; one foot lifts or slides.

**Correction.** Chapter 4, Width; Chapter 5, The squeeze without the collapse.

### Test 14 – Buttocks

**Where.** The tester places a hand on the student's buttock, on the large muscle.

**Direction.** Light pressure; at Level 3, open-hand pats.

**Levels.** 1, 2, 3.

**Checks.** Whether the gluteal muscles are engaged to hold the pelvic tuck.

**Failure looks like.** The muscle is soft; it tightens only when touched, showing that the student is not holding it on their own.

**Correction.** Chapter 5, How to tuck without strain.

### Test 15 – Knees out and in

**Where.** The tester kneels or crouches beside the student and places a palm on the inside of the knee (to push outwards) or the outside (to push inwards).

**Direction.** Horizontally outwards, then inwards, built slowly.

**Levels.** 1, 2. Never strike the knee.

**Checks.** The inward bracing of the thighs and the alignment of the knee over the foot.

**Failure looks like.** The knee moves easily; the foot rolls; the knee collapses inwards under the inward push.

**Correction.** Chapter 5, Partner Tests for the Knees and Hips.

### Test 16 – Thighs

**Where.** The tester places a hand on the front, inside or outside of the student's thigh, on the large muscles, well away from the knee and the groin.

**Direction.** Pressure; at Level 3, open-hand slaps.

**Levels.** 1, 2, 3.

**Checks.** Whether the thigh muscles are engaged and the legs braced.

**Failure looks like.** The muscle is soft; the knee bends or the leg shifts under the slap.

**Correction.** Chapter 5; Drill 5.2 Cushion Squeeze; Drill 5.3.

### Test 17 – Calves

**Where.** The tester places a hand on the back of the student's lower leg, on the calf muscle.

**Direction.** Pressure; at Level 3, light open-hand pats.

**Levels.** 1, 2, 3.

**Checks.** Whether the lower leg is engaged and the foot gripping.

**Failure looks like.** The calf is soft; the heel lifts; the foot shifts.

**Correction.** Chapter 4, The contact of the foot; Drill 4.2.

## Whole-Body Tests

In addition to the seventeen point tests, two whole-body tests check the stance as a complete structure.

**Front push.** The tester places a palm flat on the student's upper chest, below the collarbones, and pushes steadily backwards during the out-breath. The student's whole structure should absorb the push into the rear leg without rocking back or the rear heel lifting. Levels 1 and 2.

**Back push.** The tester places a palm flat between the student's shoulder blades and pushes steadily forwards. The student should absorb the push into the front leg without rocking forward. Levels 1 and 2.

These two tests are useful at the end of a testing round, as a final check that the student is standing as a unit.

## A Standard Testing Round

The following round covers the most important tests in about three minutes, with the student standing in sanchin dachi in the double block, breathing at their current stage. It is designed for Levels 1 and 2. An instructor or a trained partner can run it during or after a performance of the kata.

1. **Crown** (Test 1): two breaths.
2. **Shoulder press** (Test 2): two breaths, pressure on the out-breath.
3. **Shoulder blades and latissimus** (Tests 3 and 4): one breath each.
4. **Fist down and elbow lift** (Tests 5 and 7): one breath each, each arm.
5. **Forearm out and in** (Test 6): one breath each direction, each arm.
6. **Abdomen** (Test 10): one out-breath.
7. **Lower back** (Test 12): one breath.
8. **Hip push** (Test 13): one breath each side.
9. **Knees out and in** (Test 15): one breath each, each knee.
10. **Front push and back push**: one breath each.

Then ask the student to perform one punch-and-block cycle and carry out the **punch push-back** (Test 8) at the end of the punch.

Record the results on a simple sheet, marking each test as "held", "partly held" or "gave way". Appendix B contains a printable scorecard for this purpose. Repeated every few weeks, the round gives a clear picture of the student's progress and shows exactly where to focus their training.

::: drill
**Drill 17.1 – Test Round Practice**

*You need:* a partner, your instructor's permission and a copy of the scorecard in Appendix B.

*What to do:* Take turns to run the standard testing round on each other at Level 1, then at Level 2. The tester records the results. Afterwards, discuss them: which tests held, which gave way and what the corrections are.

*Why it matters:* Running the round trains your eye and your hands as a tester, and being tested in a consistent order gives you a reliable picture of your own structure. Students who practise it regularly tend to improve faster, because they know exactly what they are working on.
:::

::: fault
**Problem: "Every test gives way somewhere. I don't know where to start."**

*Check.* Look at your scorecard. Which tests gave way?

*Cause.* When many tests fail, there is usually one fault at the base that is undermining the rest. Most often it is the stance: feet too narrow or too long, knees not braced, pelvis not tucked. If the base is weak, the upper body has nothing to brace against.

*Correct.* Ignore the upper-body tests for a month. Concentrate on Tests 12 to 17 and the two whole-body tests, using the drills in Chapters 4 and 5. When those hold reliably, run the full round again. You may find that several of the upper-body tests now hold as well.

*Confirm.* Compare your scorecards from before and after.
:::

::: keypoints
### Chapter Summary

- The testing map sets out seventeen test points from the crown to the calves, plus two whole-body tests.
- Each test has a defined place, direction, level, purpose, sign of failure and correction.
- The abdomen and lower back are tested only by pressure and touch, never by striking. The knees are pushed, never struck.
- Most tests are suitable for Levels 1 and 2. Tests on large muscles (latissimus, upper arms, sides, buttocks, thighs, calves) can also be performed at Level 3.
- A standard testing round covers the most important tests in about three minutes. Record the results and repeat the round every few weeks.
- When many tests fail, start at the base: the stance and pelvis usually hold the key.
:::

::: practice
### Training Tasks

1. Learn the seventeen test points well enough to name each one as a partner points to it on your body.
2. Practise Drill 17.1, the test round, with a partner at Level 1 once a week for a month, then at Level 2.
3. Keep your scorecards in your training notebook and compare them every month.
4. Choose the test that gives way most often for you, and find its correction in this book. Practise that correction daily for two weeks before testing again.
5. If you teach, introduce the standard testing round to your class at Level 1, and observe which tests most students fail.
:::
