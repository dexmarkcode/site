# Further Reading

The following books are widely available and have been useful to many Goju-Ryu students. Each takes a different approach, and none agrees with every other on every detail. Read them critically, and compare what they say with your own experience and your teacher's guidance.

## Goju-Ryu and Okinawan karate

- Higaonna, Morio. *Traditional Karatedo: Okinawa Goju Ryu* (several volumes). Detailed photographic coverage of fundamentals, kata and hojo undō by a senior Okinawan teacher.
- Higaonna, Morio. *The History of Karate: Okinawan Goju-Ryu*. A history of the style and its lineage.
- McCarthy, Patrick. *Bubishi: The Classic Manual of Combat*. An annotated translation of the text from which Goju-Ryu took its name.
- Bishop, Mark. *Okinawan Karate: Teachers, Styles and Secret Techniques*. A survey of Okinawan styles and their teachers.
- Lowry, Dave. *Moving Toward Stillness*. Short, reflective essays on traditional martial training.

## Training science and the body

- Myers, Thomas. *Anatomy Trains*. The fascial-chain model referred to in Chapter 5, written for therapists and movement teachers.
- McGill, Stuart. *Back Mechanic*. Practical, research-based guidance on protecting and rehabilitating the lower back.
- Nestor, James. *Breath*. A readable popular account of breathing and its effects on health.
- Zatsiorsky, Vladimir, and Kraemer, William. *Science and Practice of Strength Training*. A standard reference on the principles of progressive loading.

## Internal training

- Kennedy, Brian, and Guo, Elizabeth. *Chinese Martial Arts Training Manuals*. Historical context for the Chinese sources of Okinawan karate.
- Chen, Wei-ming. *T'ai Chi Ch'uan Ta Wen: Questions and Answers on T'ai Chi Ch'uan*. Classic writing on rooting, yielding and pushing hands, which throws light on kakie.
