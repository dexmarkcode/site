# Before You Begin: Safety

This is a book about training the body hard, and I would not be doing my job if I did not begin with a few plain words about safety. Please read this page before you try anything in the chapters that follow, and come back to it whenever you start a new kind of training.

## Speak to your doctor first if any of these apply

The breathing and tension methods of Sanchin raise the pressure inside your chest and abdomen and, for a short time, your blood pressure. That is part of how they work. For most healthy people it is entirely safe. For some, it is not. Please seek medical advice before you begin hard breathing, isometric tension or heavy conditioning if you have, or have had:

- high blood pressure, heart disease, an irregular heartbeat or a stroke;
- an aneurysm, or a family history of aneurysm;
- glaucoma or a detached retina;
- a hernia of any kind, or recent abdominal surgery;
- pregnancy, or you have given birth in the last few months;
- osteoporosis, or a history of fractures from minor falls;
- epilepsy, fainting or dizzy spells;
- a joint replacement, or a spinal condition.

None of these necessarily rules out Goju-Ryu. Many people with these conditions train happily for years. But they will need to use the soft versions of the breathing exercises, avoid breath-holding and keep their tension moderate. Wherever that applies in the book, I have said so and given an alternative.

## Rules for every session

1. **Warm up properly.** Chapter 2 and Chapter 15 give you warm-up routines. Never begin conditioning or Sanchin tension cold.
2. **Never hold your breath under strain.** In every exercise in this book you are either breathing in or breathing out. If you notice yourself holding your breath, stop, breathe and lighten the load.
3. **Stop at sharp pain.** Ache and fatigue are normal. Sharp, stabbing, burning or electric pain is not. Neither is numbness, tingling, a headache that comes on during exercise, chest pain or visual disturbance. Stop immediately and seek advice.
4. **Build slowly.** Every progression in this book starts gently. Respect the starting point, especially in impact conditioning. The body adapts in weeks and months, not in days.
5. **Partner testing is a skill.** Whoever tests your Sanchin must learn to strike and press correctly. Chapter 6 explains how. Never allow an untrained partner to strike the head, throat, spine, kidneys or groin.
6. **Look after your equipment.** Check handles, ropes, padding and jar rims before every use. A stone that comes off its handle can break a foot.
7. **Children and older students** need adapted programmes. Chapter 15 covers both.

## A note on the word "iron"

The phrase "iron body" is traditional and colourful, but it can give the wrong impression. The aim of conditioning is a resilient body, not a damaged one. Hands that are swollen, scarred and stiff are a sign of poor training. The best-conditioned teachers I have known had hands that looked quite ordinary and joints that moved freely into old age. That is the standard to aim for.
