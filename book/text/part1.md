# Foundations of Signals and Systems {.part}

::: partintro
Before we can transform anything we need a secure working vocabulary. This first part is about signals as objects in their own right, the systems that act on them, and the one operation that ties the two together: convolution.

Chapter 1 builds fluency with elementary signals and the operations we perform on them every day. Chapter 2 asks what makes a system linear, time-invariant, causal or stable, and why those labels matter. Chapter 3 is devoted entirely to convolution, the calculation on which every transform method in the rest of the book quietly depends.

If you are tempted to skip ahead to Fourier and Laplace, resist. Nearly every mistake I see in transform problems can be traced back to a shaky time shift, a misplaced unit step or a convolution done by guesswork.
:::
