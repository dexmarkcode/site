# The Laplace Transform {.part}

::: partintro
The Fourier transform describes a system by how it treats steady sinusoids. That is ideal for filters and communication, but it struggles with signals that grow, with systems that start from a stored state, and with questions of stability. The Laplace transform removes those limits by allowing the frequency to be complex.

Chapter 7 develops the transform, its region of convergence and its properties. Chapter 8 inverts it by partial fractions and uses it to solve differential equations with initial conditions, the problem it was made for. Chapter 9 turns to systems: transfer functions, poles and zeros, stability tests, frequency response and feedback.

By the end of this part you should be able to look at a pole–zero plot and tell, without calculation, roughly what the system will do.
:::
