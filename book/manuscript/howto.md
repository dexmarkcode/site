This book is designed to be read in order, since each chapter builds on those before it. But it is also designed to be *worked*, not merely read. Here is how to get the most from it.

### The structure of each chapter

Every chapter follows the same pattern, so that you always know where you are.

- **An opening story** sets the scene and shows why the topic matters.
- **Learning outcomes** tell you what you should be able to do by the end.
- **Main sections** develop the ideas one at a time, each with explanations, diagrams and key equations.
- **Worked examples** show the methods in action, step by step.
- **Boxes** highlight practical tips, common pitfalls and key equations.
- **A chapter summary** gathers the essential points.
- **Exercises** let you practise, with answers in Appendix C.

### How to study

**Work with a pencil.** Read each worked example with paper beside you, and reproduce the working yourself. Then close the book and try it again from scratch. If you can do it without looking, you have learned it.

**Do the exercises.** The exercises at the end of each chapter are graded from straightforward to more demanding. Try every one before looking at the answers. If your answer differs, find out why: the error is usually more instructive than the right answer.

**Draw everything.** Every problem in circuit analysis starts with a clear diagram. Redraw circuits neatly, label every node and mark every chosen current direction. Many mistakes vanish once the diagram is clean.

**Check everything.** Get into the habit, from the very first chapter, of checking each answer: Is the unit right? Is the size sensible? Does a power balance work out? Chapter 17 sets out a complete checking routine.

**Build circuits if you can.** A breadboard, a handful of resistors, a few batteries and an inexpensive multimeter will let you test almost every circuit in this book at safe, low voltages. Nothing builds confidence like measuring a voltage you predicted with a pencil.

### Suggested routes through the book

**A first course in circuit analysis** should cover all eighteen chapters in order.

**Readers with some background** in basic electricity can move quickly through Chapters 1 to 3, but should not skip Chapter 4: its precise language of nodes, branches, reference directions and sign conventions underpins everything that follows.

**For revision before an examination**, the chapter summaries, the formula summary in Appendix D and Chapter 17's decision process provide a compact review.

### A note on mathematics

You will need to rearrange equations, work with powers of ten and solve simultaneous equations with two or three unknowns. Appendix B reviews elimination, substitution and Cramer's rule with worked examples. A scientific calculator with an equation-solving mode is very helpful, and Chapter 10 shows how a few lines of Python can do the arithmetic for you – but always learn to set up the equations by hand first.
