# Further Reading

The following books are widely available and have been useful to many Goju-Ryu students. They represent different lineages and points of view, and they do not always agree with each other or with this book. That is part of their value.

**On Goju-Ryu and its kata**

- Morio Higaonna, *Traditional Karatedo: Okinawa Goju Ryu*, volumes 1 to 4. A detailed photographic record of the kata and training methods of one of the best-known Okinawan lineages.
- Morio Higaonna, *The History of Karate: Okinawan Goju Ryu*. A history of the style and its teachers.
- Seikichi Toguchi, *Okinawan Goju-Ryu: Fundamentals of Shorei-Kan Karate* and *Okinawan Goju-Ryu II: Advanced Techniques of Shorei-Kan Karate*. Valuable for their discussion of bunkai and the principles of kata analysis.
- Gogen Yamaguchi, *Goju-Ryu Karate Do Kyohan*. The Japanese branch's classic textbook.

**On kata application**

- Iain Abernethy, *Bunkai-Jutsu: The Practical Application of Karate Kata*. A clear modern method for interpreting kata.
- Patrick McCarthy, *Bubishi: The Classic Manual of Combat*. A translation and commentary on the text that influenced Goju-Ryu.
- Kris Wilder, *The Way of Sanchin Kata*. A focused study of Sanchin.

**On training and the body**

- Any sound modern text on strength and conditioning, read alongside Chapter 6, will help you apply hojo undo safely and progressively.

Always read with a critical eye, test what you read on the dojo floor, and discuss it with your teacher.
