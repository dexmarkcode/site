# Glossary

Age uke
:   Rising block to protect the head.

Bubishi
:   A collection of Chinese martial arts texts that circulated in Okinawa and influenced Goju-Ryu.

Bunkai
:   Analysis of a kata movement; its basic interpretation.

Bunkai kumite
:   A prearranged partner sequence drawn from the applications of a kata.

Chi ishi
:   "Strength stone"; a weighted lever used in hojo undo.

Chinkuchi
:   Okinawan term for short, explosive power produced by alignment, compression and the drop of the body.

Chudan uke
:   Middle-level block; in Goju-Ryu, the forearm block of sanchin kamae.

Embusen
:   The floor pattern of a kata.

Gamaku
:   The area around the waist and the skilled use of its muscles to connect the legs and trunk.

Gedan barai
:   Low sweeping block. Also used as a strike, release, pull or arm bar.

Gedan shotei
:   Downward palm heel, fingers pointing down.

Go / Ju
:   Hard and soft. The name Goju-Ryu means "hard-soft school".

Heiko dachi
:   Parallel stance, feet shoulder-width apart.

Heishu kata
:   "Closed hand" kata: Sanchin and Tensho.

Henka
:   Variation of an application to suit different circumstances.

Hiji ate
:   Elbow strike. Also called *empi*.

Hikite
:   The returning hand drawn to the hip; in application usually a grip or pull.

Hojo undo
:   Supplementary conditioning exercises, usually with traditional implements.

Ibuki
:   Strong, audible breathing used in Sanchin.

Ishi sashi
:   Stone or iron padlock-shaped weight used in pairs.

Kaishu kata
:   "Open hand" kata: the classical forms Saifa, Seiyunchin, Shisochin, Sanseru, Sepai, Kururunfa, Seisan and Suparinpei.

Kaisai no genri
:   "Theory of unlocking the secrets"; principles for analysing kata.

Kake uke
:   Hooking open-hand block.

Kakie
:   Pushing-hands exercise with the wrists in contact. Also written *kakiye*.

Kansetsu geri
:   Joint kick, usually to the knee.

Kentsui
:   Hammer-fist. Also called *tettsui*.

Koken
:   Bent-wrist strike or block using the top of the wrist.

Kongoken
:   Large iron oval used for strength training.

Kosa dachi
:   Crossed-leg stance.

Kote kitae
:   Forearm conditioning with a partner.

Kuzushi
:   Breaking the opponent's balance.

Makiwara
:   Flexible striking post.

Mawashi uke
:   Circular two-handed block ending in tora guchi; closes every classical kata.

Morote
:   Two-handed, as in *morote nukite* (double spear hand).

Muchimi
:   Heavy, adhesive quality of the hands in contact.

Musubi dachi
:   Formal stance, heels together, toes apart.

Neko ashi dachi
:   Cat stance, weight mostly on the rear leg.

Nigiri game
:   Gripping jars.

Nogare
:   Soft, quiet controlled breathing.

Nukite
:   Spear hand, striking with the fingertips.

Oyo
:   Practical application of a kata movement.

Rokkishu
:   "Six hands"; the Chinese exercise on which Tensho is based.

Sagi ashi dachi
:   Heron or crane stance on one leg.

Sanchin dachi
:   The root stance of Goju-Ryu, knees pressed inward, front foot turned in.

Sanchin kamae
:   The double chudan uke posture of Sanchin.

Seisan dachi
:   A longer stance between sanchin dachi and zenkutsu dachi; also *han zenkutsu dachi*.

Shiko dachi
:   Wide, low straddle stance with knees pressed outward.

Shime
:   Testing of Sanchin by hand.

Shotei
:   Palm heel strike or push. Also *teisho*.

Tensho uke
:   Circular open-hand block rotating the palm outward.

Tora guchi
:   "Tiger's mouth"; hand shape of mawashi uke.

Tuite
:   The art of seizing and manipulating joints. Also *tuidi*.

Uraken
:   Back-fist strike.

Ura tsuki
:   Short inverted punch.

Yoi
:   Ready position.

Zenkutsu dachi
:   Forward stance.

**Alternative spellings of kata names:** Seiyunchin (Seienchin), Sanseru (Sanseiru), Sepai (Seipai), Kururunfa (Kururunha), Suparinpei (Pechurin), Seisan (Seishan).
